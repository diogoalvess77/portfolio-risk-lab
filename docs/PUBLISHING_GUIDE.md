## LinkedIn post draft

I recently built Portfolio Risk Lab, a Python project exploring portfolio optimisation and the gap between in-sample estimates and out-of-sample performance.

The baseline analysis samples 20,000 long-only allocations across five synthetic assets, constructs a 40-point efficient frontier and compares equal-weight, minimum-volatility and maximum-Sharpe portfolios on a later holdout period.

I then extended the project with a robustness layer that repeats the full training → optimisation → holdout process across 250 independent synthetic market paths and three volatility regimes. This makes it possible to examine holdout Sharpe distributions, strategy win rates, training-to-holdout estimation gaps and portfolio-weight stability rather than relying on a single simulated path.

The project includes reproducible reports, raw outputs and 22 automated tests. All committed results use synthetic data and are intended for educational research only.
